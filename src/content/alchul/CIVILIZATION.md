# Civilization

## Core principle

They are not humans in another body.

Their civilization develops from:
- predator cognition
- grasping theropod anatomy
- multimodal communication
- fermentation and chemistry
- altered-state perception
- geological sensitivity
- long-duration cultural learning
- subterranean adaptation
- Alchul contact
- later directed biological evolution

## Social cognition

The founding animals are facultatively social.

Over long spans, recurring Harvest gatherings increase:
- tolerance
- coordination
- juvenile learning
- memory transfer
- group ritual
- shared altered states
- collective problem solving

Eventually some group states function as higher-order cognition without erasing individuality.

## Communication

Possible channels include:
- vocal calls and tonal sequences
- posture
- feather/filament display
- tail and head movement
- touch
- chemical signaling
- scent marking
- substrate vibration
- environmental arrangement
- altered-state synchronization
- later direct or group-linked cognition

Writing is optional, not foundational.

A culture can preserve knowledge through:
- cultivated organisms
- chemical formulas
- spatial pattern
- rhythm
- ritual sequence
- engineered environments
- mnemonic lineages
- mineral structures
- shared cognitive states

## Religion and science

There is no clean early split.

The same specialists can be:
- observers
- healers
- chemists
- ritualists
- navigators
- geologists
- engineers
- Alchul intermediaries

Repeatability gradually turns ritual into method without eliminating sacred interpretation.

## Technical path

Their path does not mirror:
stone → bronze → iron → fossil-fuel industry → electronics.

A better lineage is:
predation → fermentation → preservation → fire → chemistry → geology → environmental engineering → altered-state science → boundary physics → displacement → living/ecological machines.

## Architecture

Early settlements are shaped around:
- hunting territory
- nesting
- observation
- controlled access
- food processing
- fermentation
- fire
- water
- gas
- ritual/contact spaces

Later underground settlements are:
- carved
- grown
- cultivated
- geological
- biological
- thermodynamic

A city may resemble an ecosystem more than a constructed skyline.

## Geological sensing

Their early geological skill is embodied.

They do not initially move stone by will.

They sense:
- where it is fractured
- where pressure is accumulating
- where water is moving
- where gas is trapped
- where heat is flowing
- where a blow, wedge, thermal shock, or fluid will propagate

This becomes a formal science and eventually interfaces with more advanced matter-boundary technologies.

## Deep environmental transformation

When they begin the Long Descent, they already possess sophisticated capability.

They transform underground environments by controlling:
- illumination
- bioluminescent organisms
- reflective/mineral surfaces
- combustion and gas light
- airflow
- humidity
- water
- temperature
- fungal and microbial systems
- food webs
- substrate chemistry
- structural stress

The famous luminous deep world is therefore partly natural and partly the result of millions of years of cultivation and engineering.

## Mature technology

At maturity, a machine may be an ecosystem.

Functions can be distributed across:
- living tissue
- fungi
- minerals
- fluids
- gases
- temperature gradients
- pressure
- chemistry
- resonance
- field effects
- synchronized minds

Their equivalent of a computer, archive, engine, city, and organism may overlap.

## Transport

Their oldest displacement ability grows from local perception.

Mature transport works by establishing correspondence between locations rather than only accelerating through intervening space.

Rock-like vessels are portable habitats and displacement systems.

The vessel is not merely a hull.

It is a place capable of becoming somewhere else.
