# Timeline

Dates are working ranges where science is uncertain. Canonic sequence is more important than exact dates until research narrows them.

## Phase 0 — Founder ecology
Approx. 80–78 Ma onward, working range.

A small-to-medium Appalachian dromaeosaur-grade population occupies the northern Mississippi Embayment region as a mesopredator in coastal-plain, river, floodplain, wetland, lacustrine, estuarine, and nearshore mosaics.

They are already capable hunters with strong place memory, sensory acuity, grasping forelimbs, juvenile learning, and facultative sociality.

## Phase 1 — The Harvest

Naturally fermenting plant resources become repeatedly exploited at localized seasonal sites.

Individuals return deliberately.

Repeated aggregation changes social tolerance, learning, juvenile exposure to adults, ritualized behavior, and selection.

## Phase 2 — First anomalous perception

A subset of Harvest participants experiences repeatable anomalies associated with altered states and particular places.

These begin as:
- fixation on apparently empty locations
- unusual dreams
- impressions or memories that do not belong to the present
- local glimpses of the same place in another time or condition
- shared or repeated patterns

Alchul is not yet understood or consistently named.

## Phase 3 — Cultural persistence

Behavior increasingly survives the individual through:
- observation
- repeated places
- ritual
- acoustic patterns
- chemical cues
- posture and display
- tool use
- environmental marking
- group memory

The population becomes culturally distinct before it becomes conventionally "civilized."

## Phase 4 — Early technical civilization

Over deep time the population develops:
- controlled and eventually clean fire
- containers
- preservation
- fermentation control
- medicine and poisons
- hunting systems
- traps and bait
- mineral selection
- stone and bone working
- chemistry
- environmental management
- more sophisticated communication

Science and religion grow from the same body of repeatable experience.

## Phase 5 — Geological sense and matter-boundary science

Certain individuals become exceptionally sensitive to:
- fractures
- pressure
- hidden water
- heat flow
- gas
- material stress
- resonance
- unstable seams

This begins as embodied perception rather than force.

They learn to exploit where matter is already close to changing.

At the same time, localized temporal/spatial perception becomes increasingly studied and reproducible.

## Phase 6 — Early other-side access

The civilization progresses from:
perception → repeatable contact → controlled interaction → limited crossing.

Some specialist lineages can pierce into the other side more reliably.

These roles combine what humans would separately call shaman, scientist, physician, navigator, and engineer.

## Phase 7 — Knowledge of the coming end

Across generations, repeated localized glimpses reveal:
- extreme light in the sky
- surface fire
- prolonged darkness
- cold
- ecological collapse
- silence where prey and forests had been

Interpretation remains contested, but the pattern becomes too consistent to ignore.

## Phase 8 — Preparation before descent

Before moving deeply underground, the civilization already possesses:
- mature fire control
- chemistry
- preservation
- organized knowledge systems
- geological sensing
- engineered habitats
- controlled altered states
- early other-side capabilities
- substantial social coordination

The deep Earth is a destination and survival project, not the origin of civilization.

## Phase 9 — The Long Descent

Migration underground unfolds slowly over many generations and populations.

Early subsurface sites are:
- refuges
- laboratories
- storage sites
- ritual/contact sites
- energy/resource nodes
- habitation experiments

They are modified continuously.

The inhabitants alter:
- light
- airflow
- water
- heat
- gas
- microbial and fungal communities
- food webs
- surfaces
- architecture
- chemistry

Natural luminous and chemically powered systems are cultivated and amplified.

Some populations remain surface-linked for long periods.
Some are lost.
Some are isolated.
Some are born entirely below.

## Phase 10 — K–Pg catastrophe

The surface world collapses.

The civilization survives below.

The surface phase ends.

## Phase 11 — Deep-world consolidation

After K–Pg, the underground population expands, links previously separated systems, cultivates larger ecologies, and makes the deep environment increasingly artificial-natural: not a constructed bunker, but a transformed living world.

## Phase 12 — Biological divergence and directed evolution

Across millions of years:
- populations diverge
- subterranean adaptations accumulate
- communication systems become richer
- Alchul-sensitive lineages change
- directed heredity becomes possible
- body plans are altered intentionally
- reproduction and development may be redesigned

The founders would no longer recognize all descendants as their own kind by appearance alone.

## Phase 13 — Mature non-local technology

The ancient perceptual phenomenon becomes engineering.

Progression:
localized perception → boundary interaction → small displacement → living displacement → habitat/system displacement → non-local transport.

Machines increasingly become complete ecosystems.

## Phase 14 — Planetary civilization

Deep settlements spread through Earth.

Geology becomes infrastructure.

Fault systems, fluids, heat, gas, mineral growth, living ecologies, and field effects become components of civilization.

New Madrid remains one of the oldest and most important ancestral regions.

## Phase 15 — Expansion beyond Earth

Rock-like vessels carry portable ecological/physical systems into space.

Asteroids, moons, planetary interiors, fractures, and subsurface environments become natural destinations.

Transport increasingly depends on displacement physics rather than conventional acceleration alone.

## Phase 16 — Human emergence

The civilization observes mammals, primates, and eventually humans.

Humans are not their descendants, creations, or copies.

Some convergence of form and behavior occurs because Earth biology faces recurring functional constraints.

Human mythology may contain distorted traces of contact, but specific traditions remain culturally distinct and are not automatically literal records.

## Phase 17 — Present

The New Madrid civilization remains active as part of a broader Type ~1.7-equivalent civilization.

Its surface signatures can resemble ordinary geology because its systems are integrated with the planet rather than placed on top of it.
