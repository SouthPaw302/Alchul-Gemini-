# Visual Canon

## Founder population

Founder forms are strongly theropod:
- feathered
- toothed
- long-tailed
- digitigrade
- three-fingered
- enlarged second-toe claw
- nonhuman posture
- predatory sensory focus

Do not depict:
- modern bird beaks
- human hands
- human torso proportions
- generic scaly movie raptors
- reptilian humanoids

## Deep-time evolution

After tens of millions of years, especially after directed biological design begins, descendants can look radically different from founders.

Rules:
- retain terrestrial biological continuity
- preserve deep anatomical logic rather than superficial founder appearance
- allow major divergence among lineages
- allow convergence on efficient upright/manipulative forms
- do not make them humans with scales
- do not freeze them as raptors in robes

## Modern New Madrid forms

At a distance some individuals may read as person-like.

Up close, anatomy should remain clearly non-mammalian and nonhuman.

Possible traits:
- refined upright posture
- reduced or vestigial tail in some lineages
- highly dexterous non-primate hands
- shortened theropod-derived face
- altered integument: skin, keratin, filaments, localized feather structures
- large expressive eyes
- nonhuman jaw and cranial geometry
- modified digitigrade or semi-digitigrade gait
- subtle ancestral scute/keratin patterns
- sensory crests or filaments
- extensive lineage-specific variation

Human observation over millions of years can influence deliberate design, but they remain their own lineage.
