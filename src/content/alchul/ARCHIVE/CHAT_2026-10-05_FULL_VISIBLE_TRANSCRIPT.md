> **Founder Research — Late Cretaceous New Madrid / Mississippi Embayment**
>
> The future New Madrid region lay near the northern Mississippi Embayment during the Late Cretaceous. The landscape shifted between nonmarine coastal plain, riverine, lacustrine, deltaic, and shallow nearshore environments.
>
> **Working founder lineage:** an unnamed Appalachian dromaeosaurid / maniraptoran population. Small-to-medium body size, feathered, predatory or opportunistically omnivorous, digitigrade, long-tailed, with an enlarged second-toe claw, three-fingered grasping forelimbs, toothed skull, binocular vision, and relatively sophisticated cognition for a non-avian dinosaur.
>
> **Feathering:** strongly supported across dromaeosaurids. The safest reconstruction is a substantially feathered animal, with pennaceous forelimb feathers, body plumage, likely feathered tail, scaled lower legs and feet, and possibly exposed facial integument around the snout and eyes.
>
> **Hands and wrists:** three functional digits, curved claws, mobile maniraptoran wrist with semilunate carpal mechanics, capable of seizing, hooking, bracing, pulling, holding prey, and manipulating objects. No modern opposable thumb at founder stage.
>
> **Reproduction and juvenile development:** related maniraptorans show nest attendance, brooding, partial egg burial, iterative laying, intermediate incubation, and meaningful parental investment. Troodontid evidence also supports communal nesting in at least some lineages. That gives us repeated nesting sites, juvenile observation, and social learning before civilization.
>
> **Social behavior:** do not assume wolf-like packs. The safer model is facultative sociality—solitary hunting, pair or kin tolerance, juvenile learning, temporary aggregation around food, nesting, water, or opportunity, possible communal nesting, and opportunistic cooperation.
>
> **Ecological role:** mesopredators, not dominant megafaunal predators. Their broader Appalachian neighbors included hadrosaurs, tyrannosauroids, ornithomimosaurs, nodosaurids/ankylosaurs, crocodilians, turtles, fishes, sharks, and marine reptiles in nearby coastal systems.
>
> **Plants, fruit, and fermentation:** eastern North American Cretaceous floras preserve abundant angiosperm flowers, fruits, seeds, and stamens. Fruiting angiosperms existed, sugary tissues created microbial niches, and yeast lineages associated with ethanol production were diversifying during the Cretaceous. So naturally fermented plant material is a plausible biological trigger for the Harvest, though the exact fruit and chemistry are still open.
>
> **Why this lineage works:** a mesopredator benefits from memory, visual attention, prediction, stealth, opportunism, rapid learning, behavioral flexibility, object manipulation, repeated use of successful places, and observation of older individuals.
>
> **Fossil-record implication:** the terrestrial Cretaceous record in the New Madrid region is poor and deeply buried beneath younger Mississippi Embayment sediments, so their absence from the exposed fossil record is not automatically problematic in the story.
>
> **Still open:** body size, prey spectrum, brain/endocast details, wrist limits, exact feather distribution, vocal communication, chemical signaling, specific plant taxa, fungi/yeasts/psychoactive compounds, climate, seasonality, and realistic subsurface routes.
