# Last Surface Age

Status: canon reconstruction constrained by `RESEARCH/LAST_SURFACE_AGE.md`.

This file describes the final transition from a dual surface/deep civilization to permanent subterranean survival. Exact dates inside the final interval remain deliberately approximate.

## Core rule

They do not abandon the surface because it already looks dead.

They abandon it while it is still visibly alive.

Dinosaurs still move through the broader Appalachian landscape. Coastal forests still reproduce. Rivers still run. Nesting seasons still occur. The sea still contains ammonites, sharks, turtles, and mosasaurs.

Only they possess evidence that this continuity is about to end.

## ~500,000 to ~100,000 years before impact

The deep network is mature but not yet dominant.

Surface civilization remains active.

Deep sites function as:
- observatories
- laboratories
- archives
- nurseries
- food-production systems
- energy nodes
- ritual/contact sites
- permanent settlements for some populations

The civilization continues to transform underground environments:
- cultivating light
- controlling air
- regulating water
- managing heat
- breeding fungi and microbial systems
- building stable food webs
- controlling gas
- integrating structure with natural rock

The deep world is already beautiful in places, but it is still expanding.

## ~100,000 to ~10,000 years before impact

Repeated temporal observations of the coming catastrophe converge.

Independent observers see versions of the same future.

Debate changes from:
"Is this real?"

to:
"When?"

Surface institutions begin transferring anything that cannot be recreated easily:
- biological diversity
- reproductive lineages
- knowledge systems
- cultivated organisms
- medicines
- fermentation cultures
- specialized ecological symbionts
- historical memory

Deep habitats are no longer refuges.

They are being prepared to become the civilization's primary world.

## Final millennia

Population policy shifts.

New permanent surface settlements are rarely founded.

Existing surface communities either:
- relocate
- become temporary stations
- become observation sites
- refuse migration

Births increasingly occur below.

Some children know the surface only through visits.

Some never see it.

Large-scale surface infrastructure is dismantled, abandoned, absorbed into ecology, or allowed to disappear.

The civilization deliberately reduces its exposed signature.

## Final centuries

The predicted event becomes a practical deadline.

The deepest habitats become closed-cycle systems.

Movement between major underground populations is tested repeatedly.

Food ecologies are stress-tested without surface input.

Lighting, air, water, temperature, and microbial balance are operated as permanent environmental systems rather than emergency systems.

The remaining surface population is increasingly composed of:
- observers
- hunters
- researchers
- maintenance crews
- cultural holdouts
- individuals unwilling to abandon ancestral territory

## Final decades

Permanent relocation is effectively complete.

Travel upward still occurs.

The surface is not forbidden.

It simply stops being home.

The civilization continues watching ordinary life above:
- dinosaurs feeding and nesting
- predators following prey
- seasonal fruiting
- coastal migrations
- storms
- rivers
- insects
- birds
- mammals

That normality makes the coming event harder, not easier.

## Final years

Surface stations become observation posts.

The most valuable living systems have already been duplicated below.

No essential population depends on returning to the surface.

Individuals still go up because:
- they study the sky
- they observe fauna
- they maintain instruments and living markers
- they visit ancestral places
- some refuse to leave

## Last permanent withdrawal

There is no single gate that closes for the whole civilization.

Different regions leave at different times.

For the ancestral New Madrid network, the final permanent surface communities withdraw late enough that the world still appears ecologically normal.

The last generation to live permanently beneath open sky is followed by generations for whom the sky becomes ancestral memory.

## Impact

The catastrophe confirms what generations of observers had seen.

The civilization is already below.

Surface observers experience the event directly in whatever stations remain.

The deep civilization receives the event through:
- seismic transmission
- pressure
- changes in groundwater
- atmospheric interfaces
- transmitted observations
- Alchul-linked perception

Their geological senses make the planet itself part of the warning system.

## After

The surface is no longer a viable civilizational environment.

This is the moment the Long Descent becomes permanent.

The surviving civilization turns inward.

The transformed underground environment is no longer preparation for another world.

It is their world.
