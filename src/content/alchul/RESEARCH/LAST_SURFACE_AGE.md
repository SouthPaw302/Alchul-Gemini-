# Last Surface Age — Latest Maastrichtian Research

Status: real-world research layer only. This file does not establish fictional canon by itself.

Scope: the final ~500,000 years before the Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) boundary, with emphasis on the Mississippi Embayment, southeastern Missouri, northern Mississippi, and the broader Appalachian landmass.

Research standard:
- **Established** = directly supported by fossil, stratigraphic, geochemical, or paleoclimatic evidence.
- **Plausible** = reasonable regional inference.
- **Unknown** = not demonstrated for the exact New Madrid locality.

## 1. Time boundary

**Established:** The K–Pg boundary is approximately 66 Ma.

The latest Maastrichtian record of the Mississippi Embayment reaches extremely close to the impact horizon.

At New Albany, Union County, Mississippi, the upper Owl Creek Formation contains ammonites, dinoflagellate cysts, and nannofossils indicating deposition during the final ~1 million years of the Cretaceous and probably the final ~500,000 years.

The overlying basal Clayton Formation contains Chicxulub impact spherules.

This provides a direct regional geological record of the end-Cretaceous event.

## 2. The northern embayment immediately before the boundary

**Established:** In southeastern Missouri the latest Maastrichtian Owl Creek Formation represents a nearshore/inner-neritic environment with strong terrestrial input.

Palynological work reconstructs mixed coastal and inland **subtropical forest dominated by angiosperms**.

The northern embayment therefore remained biologically productive immediately before the boundary.

**Established:** the ancestral Mississippi drainage system was developing by Maastrichtian time, carrying sediment southward through the embayment.

The final Cretaceous landscape was not today's Mississippi River valley, but a low coastal/deltaic embayment connected to large river systems.

## 3. Was New Madrid itself land?

This needs precision.

The exact shoreline moved through time. The future New Madrid area sat near the northern end of the embayment and experienced alternating nonmarine, deltaic, marginal-marine, and marine conditions during the Late Cretaceous.

The McNairy Sand records major fluvial/deltaic sedimentation earlier in the Maastrichtian.

The overlying Owl Creek represents a later marine transgression in much of the embayment.

**Therefore:** at the exact final boundary moment, some locations around the future New Madrid region may have been coast, marsh, estuary, shallow marine, or nearby terrestrial lowland rather than continuous dry inland forest.

A founder civilization associated with the broader northern embayment could nevertheless occupy terrestrial areas very close to the marine embayment.

Do not lock an exact shoreline around the founder site until a paleogeographic reconstruction is chosen.

## 4. Dinosaurs remained present

**Established:** non-avian dinosaurs remained present on eastern North America through the Maastrichtian.

Known Appalachian groups include:
- hadrosauroids
- tyrannosauroids
- ornithomimosaurs
- nodosaurids
- dromaeosaurids
- other small theropods

The latest terrestrial fossil record in the eastern United States is sparse compared with western North America, so precise last-occurrence dates are difficult to establish.

**Established:** a ceratopsid tooth from the late Maastrichtian Owl Creek Formation of Mississippi is the first definitive ceratopsid occurrence reported from eastern North America. Its age and setting are consistent with west-to-east faunal interchange late in the Maastrichtian after retreat of the Western Interior Seaway.

This means the final Appalachian dinosaur fauna was not static; western clades may have begun entering the east shortly before the extinction.

**Established:** hadrosaur remains are among the most commonly encountered dinosaur remains in Cretaceous marine/coastal sediments of the eastern United States.

**Important limit:** there is currently no fossil site at the exact future New Madrid location preserving a complete dinosaur community from the last years before impact.

## 5. Other animals in the final landscape

Nearshore Owl Creek deposits preserve or are associated with:
- ammonites
- bivalves
- gastropods
- crabs
- sharks
- chimaeras
- marine turtles
- mosasaurs

On nearby land, regional Appalachian faunas also included:
- turtles
- crocodyliforms
- mammals
- birds
- small reptiles
- insects
- large and small dinosaurs

So the final surface world was not visibly "emptying out" in preparation for extinction.

## 6. Vegetation before impact

**Established:** pollen from the northern Mississippi Embayment indicates mixed coastal and inland subtropical forest, dominated by angiosperms.

Reported plant affinities from Owl Creek palynology include groups related to:
- Betulaceae
- Juglandaceae
- Oleaceae
- Fagaceae
- Nyssaceae
- palms
- cycads

These are taxonomic/palynological affinities, not justification for depicting modern versions of those plants unchanged.

The visual environment should be a warm Late Cretaceous forest/coastal mosaic, not a modern southeastern forest.

## 7. Did the ecosystem know catastrophe was coming?

No evidence supports ordinary animals or ecosystems detecting Chicxulub far in advance.

The latest Maastrichtian Owl Creek record is abundantly fossiliferous directly below the boundary.

This strongly supports the narrative choice that, to ordinary eyes, the surface world remained alive immediately before the impact.

Any advance knowledge in Alchul canon comes from the civilization's anomalous temporal perception, not from a conventional ecological warning visible for thousands of years.

## 8. Immediate impact record in the Mississippi Embayment

**Established:** Union County, Mississippi preserves a K–Pg event bed 15–30 cm thick containing abundant Chicxulub impact spherules.

The bed contains marine fossils and sedimentological evidence for rapid, high-energy post-impact deposition.

**Established:** southeastern Missouri also preserves a K–Pg transition between the Owl Creek and Clayton formations. Research has interpreted basal Clayton deposits there as a high-energy event deposit associated with the Chicxulub aftermath, though exact depositional mechanisms and correlations require caution.

The Mississippi Embayment was therefore directly affected by impact fallout and major hydrodynamic disturbance.

## 9. First hours

Broad Chicxulub research supports the following sequence:

**Within minutes to hours:**
- crater excavation and collapse
- high-velocity ejecta launch
- melt spherules and shocked material distributed over large distances
- atmospheric transport of dust, soot, sulfate, and other ejecta
- enormous seismic disturbance
- oceanic tsunami generation

Models and sedimentary evidence indicate fast-moving impact material spread globally within hours.

Exact arrival times at the northern Mississippi Embayment depend on ejecta trajectory and event mechanism and should not be stated to minute-level precision without a regional model.

## 10. Heat and wildfire

Impact-generated thermal radiation and atmospheric re-entry of ejecta have long been proposed as major fire mechanisms.

A 2026 modeling study strengthens the case that fine dust could have enhanced atmospheric thermal radiation and wildfire ignition.

However, global fire intensity remains debated.

For Alchul reconstruction, severe regional heat and fire are scientifically defensible, but do not portray every square kilometer as simultaneously incinerated unless the narrative explicitly treats that as a model rather than certainty.

## 11. Darkness and impact winter

Recent modeling based on measured K–Pg dust grain sizes supports:
- fine silicate dust residence in the atmosphere potentially around 15 years
- global-average surface cooling potentially reaching ~15 °C in one modeled scenario
- near-total shutdown of photosynthetically active radiation for almost 2 years

Sulfur, soot, and fine dust likely acted together.

A 2025 empirical study estimated Chicxulub sulfur release lower than many earlier models, implying sulfur's contribution may have been smaller than previously thought. This makes fine silicate dust increasingly important in current impact-winter models.

**Established conclusion:** abrupt darkness/cooling and collapse of primary productivity are strongly supported.

**Uncertain:** exact local temperatures, duration of complete darkness, fire severity, and relative contributions of dust, sulfur, and soot.

## 12. What the final surface generation would realistically see

Before impact:
- normal seasonal landscape
- living forest and wetlands
- dinosaurs and other animals
- active marine and coastal ecosystems
- no obvious conventional warning of imminent global extinction

After impact, depending on exact location and hours elapsed:
- violent ground motion
- extraordinary atmospheric phenomena
- ejecta/spherule fallout
- possible intense heat and fires
- large water disturbances in the embayment
- rapidly deteriorating light conditions
- later severe cooling and food-web collapse

This is the scientifically strongest contrast: the world looks alive until it suddenly is not.

## 13. Limits relevant to canon

Do not state as real-world fact that:
- the exact future New Madrid settlement sat on dry ground at 66.0 Ma
- a specific dinosaur species was standing at New Madrid on impact day
- all Appalachian dinosaurs survived literally until the final day
- the Mississippi Embayment was hit by a precisely modeled tsunami height at New Madrid
- every forest burned
- total darkness lasted a fixed number of years
- ordinary fauna sensed the impact years in advance

Those details require either better evidence or explicit fictional reconstruction.
