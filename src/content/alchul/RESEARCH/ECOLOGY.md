# Founder Ecology — Late Cretaceous Northern Mississippi Embayment

Status: research layer only. This file contains real-world evidence and explicitly labeled inference. It does not establish canon by itself.

Research standard:
- **Established** = directly supported by fossil, stratigraphic, geochemical, or strong comparative evidence.
- **Plausible** = reasonable inference from regional or closely related evidence.
- **Unknown** = not presently demonstrated; do not present as paleontological fact.

## 1. Landscape

**Established:** The northern Mississippi Embayment contained middle-to-late Campanian nonmarine and marine deposits. The Post Creek Formation records nonmarine conditions; overlying deposits record marine influence. By the Maastrichtian, the McNairy Sand represented shallow-water, estuarine, freshwater, and marine environments across the embayment margin.

**Established:** The Coon Creek Formation in western Tennessee was deposited in a nearshore reentrant of Appalachia. Modern work places the type locality at about 76.8–76.0 Ma and approximately 35–60 m water depth, with freshwater runoff and shoreline influence.

**Plausible founder habitat mosaic:**
- river channels
- levees and flood basins
- muddy floodplains
- vegetated freshwater margins
- coastal woodland
- estuarine margins
- periodically exposed sandy ground
- nearby shallow marine embayments

The founder population should therefore not be written as occupying one continuous jungle biome.

## 2. Climate and seasonality

**Established at continental scale:** Late Cretaceous North America was warmer than today with reduced pole-to-equator temperature gradients. Climate cooled somewhat through the Campanian and more strongly into the Maastrichtian before later warming.

**Established:** seasonality existed. Late Cretaceous vegetation patterns were not uniformly tropical.

**Plausible for the northern Mississippi Embayment:** a warm, humid to seasonally wet coastal-plain climate with recurring floods, storms, dry intervals, and strong hydrological seasonality.

**Unknown:** precise local annual rainfall, dry-season length, and mean annual temperature for the exact founder locality.

## 3. Vegetation

**Established:** Late Cretaceous eastern North America had a distinct flora from western North America. Mississippi Embayment pollen assemblages include Normapolles-group angiosperms and other regional pollen types.

**Established:** Campanian floras from eastern North America preserve abundant angiosperm flowers, fruits, seeds, and stamens.

**Established:** Normapolles-producing plants in southeastern North America show affinities with the broader group containing Juglandaceae, Rhoipteleaceae, Myricaceae, and Betulaceae.

**Established:** magnoliid lineages and other core angiosperm groups were already diversified by the Late Cretaceous.

**Plausible founder vegetation:**
- angiosperm shrubs and small trees
- larger angiosperm trees
- conifers
- ferns and fern allies
- wetland vegetation
- fruiting understory and edge plants
- seasonally abundant seeds and fleshy reproductive tissues

Do not substitute modern southeastern U.S. forest species.

## 4. Fruiting resources and fermentation

**Established:** eastern North American Campanian floras contained true fruits and seeds.

**Established:** fruit-rich microbial niches can support ethanol-producing yeasts. Evolutionary work on Saccharomycetaceae links strong alcoholic fermentation with the emergence of sugar-rich fruit environments.

**Plausible:** fallen, damaged, waterlogged, or heat-exposed Cretaceous fruits and other sugary tissues could ferment naturally.

**Unknown:**
- exact local fruit taxon
- exact yeast species
- exact ethanol concentration
- whether a recurring natural fermentation basin existed at the future New Madrid location

The Harvest is therefore a plausible ecological construction, not a fossil-demonstrated local event.

## 5. Fungi

**Established:** mushroom-forming Agaricomycetes were already diverse by the Cretaceous. Gilled mushrooms are known from Cretaceous amber, including eastern North American material from New Jersey about 90–94 Ma.

**Established:** specialized mushroom-feeding beetles existed by the mid-Cretaceous, implying ecologically significant fungal communities.

**Established:** an ergot-like clavicipitaceous fungus, `Palaeoclaviceps parasiticus`, infected a grass floret about 97–110 Ma.

**Important limit:** the fossil proves an ergot-like fungus existed. It does not prove the fossil species produced the same alkaloids or psychoactive effects as modern `Claviceps`.

**Established from molecular-clock work:** the stem lineage of `Psilocybe` may date to approximately 67 Ma, near the end of the Cretaceous.

**Important limit:** this does not demonstrate `Psilocybe` in the Mississippi Embayment before K–Pg, nor does it demonstrate dinosaur ingestion.

For founder ecology, fungi should be abundant and chemically diverse, but specific psychoactive fungal use remains **Unknown** until a stronger line of evidence is found.

## 6. Terrestrial and aquatic prey

The exact local terrestrial vertebrate assemblage is incompletely known.

**Established regionally:** Appalachia contained both small and large dromaeosaurids during the Late Cretaceous, along with tyrannosauroids, ornithomimosaurs, hadrosaurs, armored dinosaurs, crocodyliforms, turtles, fishes, and other vertebrates.

**Established:** small carnivorous theropods existed in Late Cretaceous North America and occupied niches distinct from large theropods.

**Plausible founder prey/resources:**
- small reptiles
- juvenile vertebrates
- eggs
- fish stranded or caught in shallow water
- amphibian-sized prey where available
- large arthropods
- carrion
- small mammals
- nestlings
- possibly plant foods opportunistically

The exact proportions are **Unknown**.

## 7. Coastal food pulses

**Established:** Coon Creek and related nearshore environments had abundant marine invertebrates, fishes, sharks, marine reptiles, and terrestrial plant material entering the system.

**Plausible:** storms, floods, shoreline shifts, and strandings could create episodic resource pulses along the coastal plain.

This is useful for modeling a founder population already adapted to:
- returning to productive locations
- exploiting transient abundance
- moving between terrestrial and water-edge environments

## 8. Fire

**Established globally:** wildfire remained an important ecological process during the Cretaceous, and charcoal is widespread in many Cretaceous deposits.

**Unknown locally:** no current source in this file demonstrates a particular wildfire regime at the exact northern Mississippi Embayment founder locality.

Wildfire may exist in later ecological reconstructions, but should not yet be treated as a defining local pressure.

## 9. Ecological pressures relevant to later divergence

These are synthesis, not direct fossil claims.

A small-to-medium predatory maniraptoran in this habitat would plausibly benefit from:
- strong place memory
- rapid exploitation of temporary food resources
- recognition of flood and shoreline cycles
- object manipulation around nests and food
- flexible prey choice
- tolerance of conspecifics at concentrated seasonal resources
- juvenile learning around repeatedly used sites
- strong olfactory and visual discrimination

These pressures fit the later Harvest scenario without requiring the environment to be invented around the story.

## 10. What we should not claim yet

Do not currently state as fact that:
- the founders ate a specific named fruit
- they regularly consumed psychoactive mushrooms
- local ergot produced LSD-like effects
- the founder site had a known natural ethanol percentage
- the region had a permanent tropical rainforest
- the founders were coordinated pack hunters
- the founders were apex predators
- the founders lived permanently in caves before cultural divergence
- the New Madrid fault itself created special surface chemistry during the founder stage

Those remain separate story hypotheses unless later evidence supports them.
