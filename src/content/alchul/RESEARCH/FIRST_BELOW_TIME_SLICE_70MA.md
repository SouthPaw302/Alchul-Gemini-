# First Below — Phase 3: Early–Middle Maastrichtian Time-Slice Test (~70 Ma)

**Status:** real research and explicitly bounded inference. **NOT independently binding canon.** This pass narrows the *fictional reconstruction window* and assesses the geology around a proposed southeastern Ozark carbonate-margin frontier.

**Prepared:** 2026-11-09.
**Related files:** `CANON.md`, `TIMELINE.md`, `RESEARCH/FIRST_BELOW.md`, `RESEARCH/FIRST_BELOW_CORRIDOR.md`, `RESEARCH/SUBSURFACE.md`, `RESEARCH/LAST_SURFACE_AGE.md`, `WRITING/SETTLEMENT_02_FIRST_BELOW_WORKING_RECONSTRUCTION.md`.

## 1. The selection — ~70 Ma is a working narrative target, not a measured age for a cave

**Established:** The Maastrichtian stage spans approximately 72.1–66.0 million years before present on commonly used geological time scales. Exact numerical boundaries may be revised; the boundary definitions are stratigraphic, not an eternal decimal number [1].

**Working fictional selection:** Center first successful *continuous occupation* of a spring-margin habitat around **~70 Ma**, roughly **four million years before the K–Pg boundary**. Treat an envelope of **~71–69 Ma as a scenario-design window**, *not a confidence interval, radiometric date, or proven occupation*. This is a constraint on further reconstruction only.

**Why here:** A Maastrichtian river/deltaic landscape is regionally evidenced; it allows an already sophisticated surface civilization to experiment with permanent underground occupation long before the final surface crisis. This separates the **first permanent below-ground community** from the **much later civilizational relocation and K–Pg survival preparations**.

**Not established:** The exact first occupation date, a creator-approved calendar, or even that the fictional first successful station must lie within this window. The geological record cannot date a fictional event.

## 2. What the northern embayment was doing around this time

### A. The McNairy system gives a credible lower-landscape context

**Established:** Harrison and Litwin (1997) assign the McNairy Sand to the early-to-middle Maastrichtian and the later Owl Creek Formation to the middle-to-late Maastrichtian in the northern embayment. Underlying Post Creek nonmarine deposits and localized Coffee Sand are Campanian; their positions and extents vary [2,3].

**Established:** Potter-McIntyre, Breeden and Malone (2018) used detrital zircons from Maastrichtian McNairy sandstone (approximately 70 Ma) in southern Illinois to argue for development of south-flowing midcontinental drainage within the northern embayment system [4].

**Plausible:** Braided lowland channels, wet terraces, floodplains, distributaries, and sandy–muddy margins provide a credible environmental context for the surface side of the First Below route. This is not a reconstruction of an exact bend, delta coast, or annual flood level.

**Limit:** 'McNairy Sand' is a mapped depositional unit, not an instruction to portray a uniform sandstone wall surrounding the settlement; both marine and nonmarine facies occur regionally, and local shoreline position changes with time.

### B. Older rock does not prove a contemporaneous open cave

**Established:** Modern southeastern Missouri's Ozark flank and the Cape Girardeau–Bollinger region contain Ordovician carbonate and clastic formations with limestone, dolomite, and sandstone [5]. Regional studies document Paleozoic bedrock beneath or adjacent to Cretaceous strata, with a pre-Cretaceous weathering profile and younger sedimentary overstep [2,6].

**Plausible:** Some older carbonate-bearing higher ground could have remained accessible to Late Cretaceous animals moving from embayment wetlands toward more consolidated basin-margin rock.

**Not demonstrated:** That today's outcrop edge, any specific spring, any modern Ozark cave, or the detailed geometry in our first SVG was available and air-filled at **70 Ma**. Bedrock formation ages tell us nothing by themselves about when an open cave formed. The **Little Bear paleosol**, erosion surfaces, and uneven sediment cover warrant a geological search for candidate erosional highs, *not a claim that a cave is proven*.

### C. The late Maastrichtian is a different time slice

**Established:** Oboh-Ikuenobe et al. (2012) document *late* Maastrichtian Owl Creek palynofloras and depositional change in southeastern Missouri, including mixed coastal/inland subtropical vegetation, followed by K–Pg boundary disturbance [7].

**Implication:** Their late Maastrichtian flora and Owl Creek stratigraphy are useful for the final pre-impact period, but must **not be treated as direct evidence of the exact vegetation, shoreline or sediment cover at 70 Ma**. The species, watercourse, and rock exposure near a hypothetical early station remain partly unresolved.

Read `MEDIA/FIRST_BELOW_CROSS_SECTION.svg` as a **schematic**. Its broad wedge of Cretaceous sand/silt/clay grading toward older carbonate is compatible with the region, but the sketch is **unscaled and not stratigraphically resolved**.

A named 'Paleozoic carbonate' exposure at the northwest end of a sketched section is still *more defensible than a deep rift chasm* but is not demonstrated at one coordinate.

## 4. Biological and ecological limits for this time slice

The real paleoenvironment evidence supports warmer Late Cretaceous climates and productive terrestrial/marginal-marine environments, not a fixed catalog of species at the imaginary entrance. Do not place recognizably modern oaks, cypress swamp stands, modern mushrooms, or a modern Mississippi River channel as though an identical ecosystem existed.

The **fictional lineage** has already evolved sophisticated technical culture at this stage; do not revert the characters to nonsapient founder morphology. Yet do not copy present-day morphologies backwards across millions of years either.

**Canon carried forward:** ~three quarters of egg development is internal by the peak surface phase; the habitat must manage maternal metabolism and oxygen as well as external incubation. Details of embryo membranes, clutch size, eggshell timing and sex remain unresolved.

**Habitat's food remains predominantly surface-derived**, even if processed by underground ecologies. Chemical microbial primary production is a potentially interesting supplement, not a validated nutrition source for a sizeable resident population. The first permanently resident group is not yet independent of supply routes.

## 5. Relative chronology audit against the existing repository

The authoritative `TIMELINE.md` assigns a *sequence* rather than precise dates for cultural and technical phases; it does **not** lock a numerical date for the first year-round habitat. `LAST_SURFACE_AGE.md` describes a mature deep network already existing ~500,000–100,000 years before K–Pg, and continued surface/deep coexistence through the final millennia.

A **~70 Ma experimental permanent foothold** leaves approximately four million years for repeated local innovations, failures, multiple founding events, expansion, and eventual interlinked ecological infrastructure before the 66 Ma catastrophe.

This **does not require**:
- that catastrophe visions be the first motive for underground occupation;
- that all groups migrate underground immediately;
- that the earliest permanent settlement be completely self-sufficient;
- that the Long Descent be a single dated journey;
- that the same cave stay open and inhabited for millions of years;
- that Braided Reach itself survive unchanged until K–Pg.

**Working order:** peak technical surface culture → explorations and seasonal stations → one or more continuous surface-supported underground occupations → diversified surface/deep interaction → later extensive relocation → final K–Pg preparations → post-impact survival.

## 6. Stress-test questions before canon promotion

1. Which late Campanian–early Maastrichtian paleo-shoreline and exposures can we ground on detailed Missouri/Illinois geological maps and boreholes?
2. Could a higher-carbonate site have been above regional water level at ~70 Ma, and could seasonal flood rises be avoided?
3. Does a candidate air-filled route reflect Cretaceous paleokarst rather than a modern cave formed later?
4. How does a group from the founder civilization actually cross terrain from the northern embayment to the margin, and how does it move heavy food loads?
5. What maintained process survives a full year's wet/dry cycling, with redundancy for the most vulnerable reproductive members?
6. Is there an alternate geologically plausible route if Ozark-margin paleo-exposure fails the site-specific test?
7. Are narrative caves identified as *fictional examples* in every relevant file?
8. Has the creator approved the first permanent occupation date and named location before it enters `CANON.md`?

## 7. Research verdict

**Pass (regional):** an early–middle Maastrichtian (~70 Ma) scene on the southeastern Ozark carbonate margin has a broadly defensible geological backdrop. McNairy-system sedimentation and older basin-margin rock offer complementary environments.

**Conditional (local):** a dry, accessible, year-round habitable carbonate spring cave there is **not confirmed**. The access corridor, resident network, water table and vents remain fictional proposals.

**Canon status:** **No promotion.** Use the selected time slice only as a working research constraint for a second chronicle.
