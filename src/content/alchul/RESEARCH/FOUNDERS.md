# Founder Research — Late Cretaceous New Madrid / Mississippi Embayment

Status: active research notes. This file is not canon by itself.

Research standard:
- **Established** = directly supported by fossils or strong quantitative comparative evidence.
- **Plausible** = supported by close relatives or extant archosaur analogs, but not directly demonstrated for the founder population.
- **Unknown** = not currently established by evidence; story decisions must stay outside this file.

## Geographic and geological setting

**Established:** The future New Madrid region lay near the northern Mississippi Embayment during the Late Cretaceous. The area shifted between nonmarine coastal-plain, deltaic, riverine, lacustrine, and shallow nearshore environments as sea level and sedimentation changed.

Useful formations and regional anchors:
- Post Creek Formation — Campanian nonmarine coastal-plain deposits in southeastern Missouri / southern Illinois.
- McNairy Sand — Maastrichtian nonmarine to nearshore deposits beneath the New Madrid region.
- Chronister site, southeastern Missouri — Late Cretaceous terrestrial/lacustrine assemblage including `Parrosaurus missouriensis` and fragmentary theropod material.
- Coon Creek Formation, western Tennessee — Campanian shallow marine/coastal setting useful for reconstructing nearby Appalachian coastal ecology.

## Founder lineage

**Plausible working choice:** unnamed Appalachian dromaeosaurid / maniraptoran population.

Supported anatomical baseline:
- substantially feathered
- digitigrade hindlimbs
- long balancing tail
- enlarged second-toe claw
- three-fingered grasping forelimbs
- toothed predatory skull
- binocular vision
- relatively sophisticated brain for a non-avian dinosaur
- high activity physiology

The exact species, mass, and body length are **Unknown** and should remain unresolved until better regional evidence appears.

## Feathering

**Established for Dromaeosauridae broadly:** direct feather preservation and/or quill knobs occur across major dromaeosaurid clades. A heavily feathered founder is much better supported than a scaly movie-raptor reconstruction.

**Plausible for the founder population:**
- pennaceous feathers on forelimbs
- body plumage over most of the torso
- feathered tail
- scaled lower legs and feet
- exposed facial integument around parts of the snout and eyes

## Brain and sensory biology

### Olfaction

**Established for dromaeosaurids:** quantitative work on olfactory bulb proportions found dromaeosaurids had larger-than-predicted olfactory ratios for theropods of similar body size, implying strong reliance on smell.

Likely ecological uses proposed in the literature:
- locating food
- navigation over large home ranges
- operating when visual cues were reduced

This matters because the founder lineage should not be written as a purely visual predator.

### Vision

**Established at clade level:** dromaeosaurids had strong stereoscopic vision compared with many other non-avian theropods, consistent with predatory depth perception.

### Hearing and balance

**Established for `Velociraptor mongoliensis`:** CT reconstruction of the endocranium and inner ear indicates agility, strong balance, and sensitivity to a relatively wide and high auditory frequency range, estimated around 2.4–4.0 kHz for the modeled taxon.

**Plausible for the founder population:** an agile predator with strong vestibular control and useful acoustic sensitivity.

Do not copy exact `Velociraptor` thresholds into canon for an unknown Appalachian species.

## Hands, wrists, and claws

**Established:** dromaeosaurs had three functional manual digits with curved claws and a semilunate-carpal wrist architecture characteristic of maniraptoran theropods.

**Established from biomechanical work:** dromaeosaurid manual claws were mechanically well suited to puncturing and gripping loads; some studies also support climbing/scansorial capability.

**Plausible founder abilities:**
- seize and restrain prey
- hook and brace objects
- pull and hold food
- carry small objects
- manipulate environmental materials

**Unknown:** precision grip, thumb opposition, fine rotation equivalent to a primate hand.

Do not depict a modern opposable thumb at founder stage.

## Pedal sickle claw

**Established:** digit II had an enlarged, hyperextensible claw.

Biomechanical modeling of `Deinonychus` supports a strong grasping/restraint function, especially for prey smaller than the predator, more strongly than the popular image of a slashing weapon.

## Reproduction and juvenile development

Related maniraptorans provide strong evidence for a mixed reptile-bird reproductive system.

**Established for troodontids:**
- iterative egg laying
- two functional reproductive tracts in at least some forms
- partly exposed/open nest architecture
- brooding or nest attendance
- intermediate incubation strategy
- precocial young possible

**Established/strongly supported across troodontids and oviraptorids:** substantial parental care existed before modern birds.

One comparative study found clutch size and adult histology consistent with paternal care in troodontids and oviraptorids, though exact care systems remain debated.

**Plausible for the founder population:**
- repeated nesting sites
- prolonged nest attendance
- juveniles spending enough time near adults for observational learning

## Social behavior

Do not write stable wolf-like pack hunting as fact.

**Established evidence against the default pack-hunter model:** stable-isotope work on `Deinonychus` found different juvenile and adult diets and interpreted the pattern as evidence against mammal-style cooperative pack hunting.

**Plausible founder baseline:**
- solitary hunting common
- pair or kin tolerance
- parental association
- temporary aggregation around food, nesting, water, or opportunity
- opportunistic cooperation
- possible communal nesting in some closely related maniraptoran systems

This gives the Harvest a real evolutionary role if sustained aggregation later becomes more common.

## Communication

### Acoustic signaling

**Established for living archosaurs:** both birds and crocodilians possess rich acoustic communication systems. Crocodilians use calls in courtship, conflict, and parental contexts.

**Plausible for non-avian maniraptorans:** a meaningful vocal repertoire, especially in nesting, territorial, mating, and juvenile-contact contexts.

The exact sound-producing anatomy of the founder population is **Unknown**.

### Chemical signaling

This requires careful wording.

**Established in living archosaurs:** crocodilians use chemoreception extensively and possess gular/paracloacal glands suspected or demonstrated to function in social signaling. Birds also use chemical cues; uropygial secretions can encode species, sex, individual identity, and reproductive information.

**Plausible:** chemical communication could have existed in a non-avian maniraptoran lineage.

**Unknown:** whether the founder population possessed a specific glandular chemical language. This must not be presented as fossil fact.

## Plants, fruit, and fermentation

**Established:** eastern North American Campanian floras preserve abundant angiosperm flowers, fruits, seeds, and stamens. Specific Campanian fossil fruits from central Georgia include early fagaceous forms.

**Established:** modern fruit-associated yeasts rapidly convert fruit sugars to ethanol.

**Established from evolutionary work:** key yeast alcohol-dehydrogenase systems are ancient, and reviews link the evolution of strong alcoholic fermentation in yeasts with the rise and diversification of sugar-rich fruits.

Therefore:
- naturally fermented plant material in the Late Cretaceous is **Plausible to strongly supported**
- the exact fruit, yeast species, ethanol concentration, and local New Madrid chemistry are **Unknown**

Do not name a modern fruit in canon without fossil support.

## Ecological role

The founders are best treated as mesopredators rather than apex megafaunal predators.

Likely neighboring groups in broader Appalachia:
- hadrosaurs
- tyrannosauroids
- ornithomimosaurs
- nodosaurids / ankylosaurs
- crocodilians
- turtles
- fishes
- marine reptiles and sharks in nearby coastal/marine settings

## Why this lineage is scientifically promising

A dromaeosaur-grade mesopredator already combines:
- binocular vision
- strong olfaction
- agile vestibular control
- grasping forelimbs
- prey restraint with hands/feet
- nest attendance
- juvenile exposure to adults
- behavioral flexibility

Those features do not prove a path toward civilization. They do provide a better real biological starting point than a generic "smart dinosaur."

## Burial / fossil-record implication

The local terrestrial Cretaceous record is poor and heavily buried beneath younger Mississippi Embayment sediments. An endemic population in this region could be difficult to recover paleontologically.

Later cultural treatment of dead individuals is an in-universe possibility and therefore belongs in canon, not in the scientific evidence section.

## Research questions still open

- founder body mass and total length
- exact Appalachian dromaeosaur affinities
- prey spectrum
- visual acuity estimates specific to eastern forms
- precise hearing range
- forearm pronation/supination limits
- maximum object-manipulation envelope
- actual vocal anatomy
- direct evidence of chemical signaling in non-avian theropods
- local Campanian-Maastrichtian plant taxa near the northern Embayment
- local fungi and yeasts
- natural ethanol concentrations in Cretaceous fruit systems
- psychoactive/toxic fungi or plants in the region
- local climate and seasonality
- realistic routes into shallow subsurface environments
