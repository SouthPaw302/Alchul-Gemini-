## 1. Selected corridor: northwestern margin / southeastern Ozark carbonate belt

**Decision for working reconstruction:** prioritize the **southeastern Ozark carbonate-margin belt northwest/west of the northern Mississippi Embayment**, with **modern Cape Girardeau–Bollinger–northern Scott County** as a present-day *research-location guide*, not the identity of an ancient shoreline or specific cave. From settlements of the Braided Reach kind, scouts follow established terrestrial and river-connected routes toward older, better-consolidated carbonate-bearing highlands.

**Evidence — established:** In the modern southeastern Missouri area, the Mississippi Embayment occupies a sediment-filled region next to Paleozoic carbonate and clastic rocks on the Ozark margin. The Cape Girardeau–Bollinger study region contains Ordovician limestone, dolomite and sandstone; the Benton Hills record a thin younger sedimentary cover above much thicker Paleozoic carbonates [1, 2, 3].

**Evidence — established:** The broader northern embayment was tectonically and topographically reworked during Late Cretaceous time. A pre-existing uplift/subsiding structural high and progressively deposited Cretaceous clastics do *not* justify projecting modern relief or river courses backwards unchanged [4].

**Inference — plausible:** A Late Cretaceous route from coastal-plain habitat into consolidated Paleozoic carbonate at or beyond the basin margin offers a more mechanically and hydrologically credible exploration frontier than a direct descent into the deeply buried Reelfoot Rift axis.

**Unknown:** The precise Late Cretaceous outcrop position, surface slope, spring location, cave-air opening, stratigraphic age of any local void, hydraulic head, groundwater chemistry, Cretaceous subsidence history at an individual site, and distance from the fictional Braided Reach.

**Crucial distinction:** *The age of carbonate bedrock is not the age of a cave.* The study region has ancient limestone/dolomite, but a particular present-day cave passage may have formed far later. No actual 66 Ma air-filled passage has been verified for our site.

## 2. Why the basin edge matters

Harrison and Litwin (1997) distinguish Campanian nonmarine Post Creek deposits, localized marine Coffee Sand equivalents, overlying Maastrichtian McNairy Sand, and later Maastrichtian Owl Creek sediment at the northernmost embayment [5]. Their study also records marine incursions and possible uplift/erosion intervals. This demonstrates **a changing depositional interface**, not a permanent modern Mississippi valley.

Oboh-Ikuenobe and colleagues (2012) describe the late Maastrichtian southeastern Missouri succession and evidence for coastal/inland subtropical vegetation [6]. This anchors the **regional** coexistence of coastal plain and inland habitats in later Cretaceous time. It does not identify our fictional colony.

**Consequence for design:** Draw a **generic, time-dependent wedge of coastal-plain sand, silt and clay** overlapping an irregular Paleozoic carbonate-rock surface toward the embayment. An exact stratigraphic label such as “Owl Creek” is allowed only in a time slice and geographic sector where that unit actually exists. Until a date is chosen, don't depict a complete Post Creek → McNairy → Owl Creek stack as if all existed together from the start.

**Do not backdate:** modern Crowley's Ridge relief; current Mississippi and Black River channels; post-K–Pg Clayton, Porters Creek, Wilcox and Quaternary sediments; a modern state highway; post-Cretaceous faults observed in Benton Hills; modern tourist-cave passages or speleothems.

## 4. Carbonate cave mechanics — what can actually be used

**Established modern analogue:** Ozark limestone and dolomite dissolve along fractures and bedding surfaces, and cave passage development can be controlled by stratigraphic boundaries, including relatively less permeable sandstone beds [9,10]. Water-filled passages can feed springs; accessible dry chambers depend on a local water table and landscape evolution [11].

**Plausible in Late Cretaceous:** long-exposed carbonate on a basin margin may sustain some underground water movement, dissolution cavities and springs. Geological periods of uplift/subsidence and changes in water base level would modify them.

**Not established:** a convenient dry natural chamber with two independently ventilated passages and a sheltered perennial spring all in exactly the same accessible cliff in the selected belt.

For the First Below, **make those conveniences a fictional candidate condition, not a regional scientific fact**. A real paleo-site validation would require lithologic maps, Cretaceous unconformities, paleo-dates of fills/speleothems where available, and cross-sections.

## 5. A defensible two-scale geological section

The figure `MEDIA/FIRST_BELOW_CROSS_SECTION.svg` is a **schematic, not a surveyed or scaled geological profile**. It shows:

### Panel A: regional margin concept (NW → SE)

- An older exposed / near-exposed **Paleozoic carbonate bedrock margin** on the northwest.
- A **Cretaceous sand–silt–clay wedge** increasing toward the lower basin, with its exact thickness and upper boundary deliberately unquantified.
- A transition to **lowland/coastal/marginal marine** conditions which can move through time.
- A proposed **surface route from settlement territory toward rock**.
- A proposed **small spring-margin habitat**, not a city-sized cavity.
- No Paleogene or Neogene units, because these sediments did not exist at the proposed period.

### Panel B: local candidate habitat (conceptual enlargement)

- Rock fracture and bedding planes as groundwater conduits.
- Recharge entering higher ground; a distinct spring discharge lower down.
- A **hypothetical** dry chamber above the normal saturated zone.
- Two **hypothetical** potential air connections, separately routed *at the surface* but not demonstrated independent underground.
- A flood-rise limit, isolated work pocket, elevated nursery, and a bypass/escape path.
- All human-designed functional features explicitly labeled **working-fiction**.

**No numerical depth, horizontal distance, shoreline coordinate, slope, cave volume, dates or measured water table** are supplied. Any such numbers now would be invented.

### Why two scales?

The regional margin is a geological decision. The local ventilation/hydraulic network is a colony engineering hypothesis. Placing them on a single apparently measured cross-section creates false certainty. The figure separates them.

## 6. Proposed settlement evaluation protocol

Before a named local site becomes canon, answer these tests in order:

1. **Ancient map test:** Does a selected Late Cretaceous time slice actually expose carbonate or altered carbonate close to the relevant reconstructed region?
2. **Cave-age test:** Is a dry passage at that date merely possible, or supported by paleo-karst indicators? No modern-cave name can settle this.
3. **Recharge test:** Where does the water originate? Are multiple paths really distinct, or does one upstream flood affect both?
4. **Air test:** Can surface-connected openings sustain gas exchange through seasonal reversals without a single-point failure?
5. **Flood and slope test:** Is the nursery above a conservative high-water margin, with an escape route that works for egg-bearing residents?
6. **Food-energy test:** How does living organic carbon reach the station through seasons and disruption?
7. **Material-work test:** What is actually excavated/braced, and what must remain inaccessible because the rock is unsafe?
8. **Travel test:** Is a traversable surface-and-spring corridor plausible for the lineage's nonhuman movement and carried loads?
9. **Chronology test:** Is this an early permanent station, rather than the far-later final K–Pg evacuation?
10. **Canon test:** Can we separate the real strata from the imaginary cavities in one sentence?
