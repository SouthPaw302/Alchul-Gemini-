# The First Below — A Habitable Underground Frontier

Status: **real-world research and explicitly labeled inference**. This file does not independently establish fictional canon. It constrains the working settlement reconstruction in `WRITING/SETTLEMENT_02_FIRST_BELOW_WORKING_RECONSTRUCTION.md`.

Prepared: 2026-11-09. Focus: the earliest *continuously occupied* subterranean footholds created by the mature Late Cretaceous surface civilization, **well before** its final K–Pg withdrawal.

## Reading protocol

- **Established** means directly supported by studies of geology, cave physics, biology, hydrogeology, or modern analogues.
- **Plausible extrapolation** means compatible with known processes, but not demonstrated at the proposed Late Cretaceous site or in the fictional lineage.
- **Fictional design hypothesis** means a proposed Alchul-universe reconstruction that still requires creator approval before promotion to `CANON.md`.
- **Unknown** means a gap we should not disguise with an attractive illustration or precise date.

A modern example does not prove an identical feature existed in the Maastrichtian. Ancient rock age does not equal cave age. An existing cave microbial ecosystem does not imply a macroscopic food base adequate for a city.

## 1. The actual geological route: away from the buried rift axis

**Established.** The future New Madrid region is associated with a deeply buried, faulted Reelfoot Rift system beneath Mississippi Embayment sediments. Its faults and rock discontinuities are not an exposed canyon or a ready-made walking passage. USGS identifies the deeply buried fault system and the sedimentary cover; the regional rock framework includes Paleozoic carbonate strata and older basement [1, 2].

**Established.** The Ozark Plateaus are an important carbonate-karst region. In limestone and dolomite, dissolving groundwater can enlarge joints and bedding-plane openings, creating springs, water-filled passages, dry air-filled passages, sinkholes, and discontinuous networks. USGS studies of the Salem Plateau show that stratigraphic boundaries and sandstone confining beds strongly influence conduit placement [3, 4].

**Established.** Parts of the Ozark karst landscape have an extended history. Analysis of sediment-filled depressions and paleokarst supports protracted exposure and karst development; it does **not** precisely map the caves available in a particular Cretaceous season [5].

**Plausible extrapolation.** First exploration runs outward from the productive embayment settlements toward firmer, fractured, carbonate-bearing country along the older margins and adjoining highlands. Select actual Late Cretaceous outcrop and shoreline settings only after a dedicated paleogeographic site-selection pass. Current Ozark river names, present cave entrances, and modern cave layouts cannot simply be backdated 66 million years.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** The earliest successful site is a *spring-margin complex*, several linked but imperfectly connected pockets at modest depth, with one main air-bearing chamber above the active groundwater level, a protected intake, and two separable atmospheric paths. It is a node in a route, not an access shaft plunging into the rift. The precise location, relief, depth, cave shape, and distance from Braided Reach remain undecided.

**Reject:** a direct open corridor from a New Madrid surface city to crustal-depth megacaverns; faults as effortless hollow tunnels; a modern tourist cave mapped unchanged into the Cretaceous.

## 2. The cave must be surveyed as a living hydraulic system

**Established.** Groundwater in Ozark carbonate rock can move rapidly through sinkholes, caves, and enlarged fractures, while other pathways are much slower. Recharge can come from rainfall infiltration and losing streams; discharge occurs through springs and streambeds [6]. A spring's visible outlet does not by itself reveal all upstream recharge or contamination routes.

**Established.** Karst caves are not static rooms. Channels flood, sediment fills openings, rock dissolves, roofs fail, and previously dry passages may become water-filled after weather or base-level changes [3, 6].

**Plausible extrapolation.** Candidate survey must span multiple wet and dry cycles and include upstream land observation, not merely a short cave visit. A skilled sensing lineage may notice the earliest changes in pressure, vibration, air current, seepage, and substrate response; those senses improve *inspection* but do not replace physical tests.

### Proposed pre-occupation tests (fictional engineering, not recovered prehistoric methods)

1. **Water:** Map where spring water enters and exits; distinguish permanent flow from short-lived flood pulses. Isolate drinking water from settlement wastewater and fermenting cultures.
2. **Air:** Establish more than one physically independent air route, measure flow in warm and cool seasons, identify stagnant pockets, and track unexplained changes in odor, breathing response, and flame behavior. Flames are never the sole safety test.
3. **Rock:** Record joints, bedding planes, unstable roofs, fall debris, dissolution cavities, slope, groundwater undercutting, and zones requiring either reinforcement or abandonment.
4. **Exposure:** Track flood levels, water chemistry, sediment, seasonal biological resources, and movement routes from the surface.
5. **Escape:** Test evacuation when either the main entrance, main stream, or main ventilation channel is blocked.

**Critical criterion:** a chamber is not suitable merely because one visit finds it dry and breathable.

## 3. Air is the first non-negotiable infrastructure

**Established.** Cave airflow can be driven by density differences between inside and outside air (the chimney effect), barometric variation, and wind. Flow direction and intensity depend on elevation differences, passage geometry, temperature, and external conditions; seasonal reversals occur [7, 8].

**Established.** Cave CO2 can vary substantially through time and space; high CO2 and low oxygen are serious biological constraints, and sulfidic or gas-charged environments may bring additional toxicity [8, 9].

**Plausible extrapolation.** The first deep residences will use surface-connected intake and exhaust systems, geometry and temperature differentials, protected vertical offsets where available, and cautiously controlled heat or draft. A colony cannot responsibly occupy its chambers on the assumption that one narrow vent will always breathe in the needed direction.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** The first settlement has three atmospheric zones:

- **Outer exchange:** easily ventilated transition spaces for material processing and returning visitors.
- **Inner habitation:** humidity, temperature, and draft moderated through rock mass, screens, channels, and regulated openings.
- **Isolatable hazard/work zones:** fermentation, combustion, selected chemistry, microbial cultivation, and unfamiliar springs are separated from nurseries and sleeping grounds.

Airflow paths are inspected continuously through embodied and maintained instruments; environmental signs are recorded in more than one independent medium. The design must have an answer for vent obstruction, rising CO2, smoke backflow, flood-driven pressure, and a failed exhaust outlet.

**Reject:** sealed populated caves without credible oxygen/carbon dioxide control; unvented fire next to nurseries; bioluminescence presented as a source of breathable oxygen; unrestricted fire use in unknown gas pockets.

## 4. Water: sufficient quantity is not sufficient quality

**Established.** A flowing spring depends on upstream recharge and aquifer geometry. Rapid subsurface movement can carry contaminants; changing precipitation alters flow and chemistry [3, 6].

**Plausible extrapolation.** Springs supply drinking, washing, thermal buffering, chemical treatment, and perhaps mechanical work, but each use needs a distinct pathway. The settlement must account for surface-derived pathogens/organic contamination, mineral chemistry, sediment loads, flood backflow, and wastewater disposal.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** A protective hydraulic design has:
- an upstream protected intake or controlled withdrawal point;
- settling and treatment areas outside nursery air;
- a retained reserve if the primary supply becomes turbid or contaminated;
- separate return channels for habitation wastewater, work-area discharges, and seasonal flood overflow;
- removable/repairable screens; no reliance on a universal self-cleaning living filter.

Water changes the city before stone is remodeled. Its earliest architecture is made from *where the water is permitted to travel*.

## 5. Heat: rock is a reservoir, not a free furnace

**Established.** Modern geothermal gradients vary. A USGS synthesis mapped gradients broadly ranging ~15–35 °C/km over most of the conterminous United States, with an eastern US mean near ~25 °C/km. These are contemporary measurements and cannot be assigned uncritically to a Late Cretaceous micro-site [10].

**Plausible extrapolation.** At modest cave depth, surrounding rock buffers short-period temperature swings. That helps with thermal stability but does not provide unlimited usable heat or food energy. Water may transport heat in or out, and deeper warm-water sources require specific geological demonstration.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** Early inhabitants use thermal mass, water routing, insulated biological materials, and carefully exhausted combustion to regulate activity and egg-development spaces. The true local thermal regime is left unspecified until a geological model is selected.

**Reject:** limitless geothermal power at a shallow spring; convenient hot vents in every chamber; heat treated as interchangeable with food calories.

## 6. Energy, food, and the decisive metabolic problem

**Established.** Most cave food webs are resource-limited and draw heavily on surface-derived organic carbon, including detritus washed or carried inward. Analyses of cave streams show that resource supply can constrain higher consumers [11, 12].

**Established.** Some microbial communities do produce biomass through chemical energy. Movile Cave in Romania is an example of a specialized chemoautotrophic ecosystem associated with sulfidic and methane-bearing waters. A modern study also shows trace-gas-consuming microbial primary production in aerated caves [13, 14]. These examples prove microbial *possibility*, not widespread calorie abundance adequate for a sizeable intelligent predator-descended settlement.

**Plausible extrapolation.** During the first continuously occupied phase, most net food and useful biomass still originate in surface photosynthesis, imported preserved matter, and cultivated organisms fed by those imports. Subsurface chemical production, recycling and symbioses initially supplement rather than replace this input.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** Separate the food economy into:
- **surface-linked energy:** fresh and preserved food, fuel, imported plant/fungal substrates, nutrient cultures, and food-bearing return routes;
- **underground conversion:** fermentation, fungal and microbial processing, safe mineral/chemical work, waste recycling, edible secondary biomass when biochemistry permits;
- **experimental chemical production:** controlled biofilms using verified local redox chemistry, initially treated as low-yield research rather than a civilizational food miracle.

No closed-loop biosphere can avoid an energy source and finite nutrient losses. 'Recycling' is not 'creating calories'. A culture that consumes fuel or imported organic substrate remains coupled to surface productivity even when its equipment is entirely underground.

### A basic accounting discipline

For each candidate habitat, require a recorded balance of **incoming food/usable chemical energy**, **respiratory demand**, **water intake and discharge**, **waste/nutrient loss**, **fuel**, and **reserve duration**. Do not assign population numbers until these systems have been bounded. Avoid ungrounded capacity figures based solely on chamber floor area.

## 7. Reproduction makes habitat quality measurable

**Established real-world analogy.** Reptile studies show oxygen supply strongly constrains prolonged egg retention; the later in-development embryo can be especially sensitive to hypoxia. Major vascular and gas-exchange adaptations are needed for advanced internal development [15, 16].

**Canon premise, not real paleontology.** The fictional peak-era lineage internally retains developing eggs for approximately three quarters of embryonic development before laying.

**Plausible extrapolation.** The internal stage raises the oxygen and nutritional stakes for egg-bearing parents; final external incubation also requires a reliable atmosphere, temperature, humidity, and contamination control. A shelter fit for healthy adults may still be unsafe for egg-bearers or late-stage eggs.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** A successful permanent station needs a nursery adjacent to the cleanest stable air and the most reliable escape/food routes. A trial that cannot sustain gestation and egg-laying across seasonal changes remains an outpost, not yet a multigenerational community.

**Still unknown:** actual oviduct vascularity, shell timing/permeability, embryonic membranes, gas exchange efficiency, egg size, clutch number, hatchling condition, sex and parental labor systems. Do not extrapolate precise values from lizards onto fictional maniraptorans.

## 8. Culture, cognition, and architecture remain nonhuman

**Existing canon.** These are not minimally changed founders of ~80 Ma nor present-day descendants. By the first sustained deep settlement they are a mature, evolved, nonhuman civilization. The exact intermediate morphology is unsettled.

**Plausible extrapolation.** Built spaces should support their actual balance and locomotion, visual/scent/tactile/vibrational cues, egg-bearing movement constraints, and communal but non-homogeneous social organization. The functions may resemble a laboratory, nursery, kitchen, archive, and clinic to us, while the geometry does not resemble human rooms or streets.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** The first permanent site divides work into overlapping groups: current and pressure readers, air tenders, cultivators, nursery guardians, wayfinders, structural workers, empirical observers, and knowledge custodians. Some individuals serve in multiple capacities. Authority is situational and contestable rather than assigned an invented monarchy or caste.

**Existing canon.** Information can be maintained through trained memory, material pattern, chemical sequences, acoustics, tactile marks, cultivated ecologies, and later shared perception. None is automatically a magical hard drive.

## 9. Light and the eventual visual transformation

**Established.** Ordinary cave interiors beyond the entrance are unlit by sunlight. Some life is bioluminescent, but an extensive naturally self-lighting continental cave forest is not an established ecological finding. Chemosynthesis is not itself a lighting system.

**Plausible extrapolation.** Initial illumination may be local, costly, and intermittent: safely vented flame, controlled chemical light where chemistry permits, light piped/reflected from accessible entrances, and cultivated luminous organisms only if specifically developed and supported by a realistic energy budget.

**Fictional design hypothesis.** The first generation is not surrounded by a perfected sapphire-green luminous paradise. Darkness remains functional; residents rely on evolved perception, tactile route information, and narrowly allocated light. Generations of engineering can later turn lighting into an ecological property of the habitat.

**Reject:** a complete deep luminous ecosystem at first contact; bioluminescent fungi that also inexplicably generate their own food energy from darkness.

## 10. A tiered historical transition, not a single descent

The following **relative stages** are compatible with existing canon; none has a locked absolute date:

**A. Surface-run exploratory routes.** Specialists visit springs, fractures, and isolated voids. They map water and air, establish caches, and return.

**B. Seasonal work stations.** Experimental cultivation, mineral study, and controlled-contact observation occur below. Surface supply is routine; disaster exits exist.

**C. Continuous occupation.** Small resident groups stay through wet/dry cycles and maintain permanent environmental systems. The station is **not** self-sufficient.

**D. Reproductive continuity.** Some young are born, incubated, and raised there. Biological needs and childhood learning reshape architecture.

**E. Linked habitats.** Reliable routes connect other carbonate-edge and fracture-fed stations. Knowledge, reproductive lineages, food, and organisms are deliberately duplicated.

**F. Partial autonomy.** Some nodes become able to survive longer supply interruptions, though complete closure is not assumed. Much later the same engineering logic supports the large Long Descent networks.

**G. Final-surface crisis.** Only after much more history do repeated observations of the approaching K–Pg catastrophe turn an old dual-world civilization into a deliberate relocation program.

This ordering must not erase the extensive period when surface and underground communities coexist.

## 11. Candidate-site comparison (qualitative, not a surveyed map)

| Site class | Primary advantage | Primary risk | Early priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air-bearing carbonate margin above active spring | Existing voids, accessible water, possible multiple vents | Flooding, collapse, variable CO2, contaminated recharge | **Preferred fictional first permanent habitat** |
| Shallow fracture or spring chamber in firmer non-carbonate rock | Stable contact with older rock and mineral resources | Small voids, poor connectivity, water/gas hazards | Exploration and specialized work |
| Sediment-dominated floodplain hollow near Braided Reach | Close logistics, familiar setting | Unstable walls, flooding, shallow groundwater | Temporary cache, not primary refuge |
| Deep rift-axis fracture system | Geochemical interest and later technical value | No established traversable void network, pressure/heat complexity | Much later, mediated access |

This comparison is an **engineering judgment for fictional reconstruction**, not a claim that an actual Cretaceous cave of each type has been found.

## 12. Failure cases the narrative should not skip

- The primary vent reverses during a seasonal temperature shift.
- A flood routes sediment into an air exhaust and changes pressure elsewhere.
- A 'clean' spring receives contamination from an unexpected recharge pathway.
- An imported food culture loses productivity in damp air or foreign substrate.
- Chemical production supports microbes but cannot feed the residents it was expected to feed.
- Egg-bearers show physiological stress before other inhabitants notice a dangerous atmosphere.
- A route that works for agile scouts cannot safely evacuate juveniles or heavily burdened egg-bearers.
- A biological screen grows, clogs, fails, or becomes an unintended microbial habitat.
- Two observers disagree whether an Alchul-linked impression is a temporal anomaly or exceptional ordinary inference.
- A successful station survives only because surface partners repeatedly rescue it.

These are proposed story tests that preserve physical consequence and avoid Alchul as a rescue device.

## 13. Strict promotion gates before new canon

A specific first settlement should not be locked until we can answer:

1. What Late Cretaceous bedrock and paleogeographic margin makes access plausible?
2. What is the relative chronology between peak surface civilization, Braided Reach-like settlements, first year-round underground occupation, and the much later K–Pg countdown?
3. How many separate air routes remain usable under seasonal reversal and blockage?
4. What surface-derived inputs are necessary, and how long can resupply stop without catastrophe?
5. Which functions are genuinely subterranean production versus surface-fed conversion?
6. What evidence makes the nursery safe for advanced egg retention and external incubation?
7. What failures have been observed, and how did residents change the design?
8. What sensory/body-plan details must be derived from the evolved surface lineage rather than founder or modern visual models?
9. How is knowledge copied when people or biological archives die?
10. Which details remain unknowable to residents, including the true nature of Alchul?

## 14. Working conclusion

**The First Below is not the first discovery of a magnificent hidden world. It is the first proof that the surface civilization can deliberately sustain a permanent, multigenerational pocket of life where sunlight does not reach.** The achievement lies in controlling flows, uncertainty, and reproduction while keeping the settlement *honestly dependent* on its support network. That dependence supplies the technical and moral problem that later generations must solve before the final surface withdrawal.
