# Five Distances Around Tesh

**Status:** working observational scenes about one invented mature-surface adult; not locked history.
**Setting:** an undated mature surface settlement of the Braided Reach type. These scenes accompany `LORE/INDIVIDUAL_01_TESH_BODY_AND_LIFE.md` and the interactive `MatureSurfaceBodyLab`.
**Translation:** spoken text represents acoustic, postural, tactile, chemical and spatial communication; it is not a literal English-speaking species.

## 1. The distance from which an animal becomes a person

Seen from across a river margin, Tesh is a moving mass with an attentive head and an elongated balance line. The whole body turns before the feet travel very far. One limb advances while the other is already testing the next place to bear weight. The forelimbs are carried close enough to protect their working digits.

A human observer might see only a small predator descended from predators.

The animal resemblance is not false. The mistake is imagining that it explains the entire behavior.

Tesh has not approached the wet bank to kill anything. Another worker waits farther downstream, nearly hidden behind a standing growth. The two are conducting an experiment. Each has a copy of yesterday's record and neither has been allowed to inspect the other's present observations.

Tesh holds one forelimb near a prepared wooden stave and rests three digit tips against different points of its upper surface. The stave has been set where water transfers movement through ground that appears firm. Tesh adjusts one of the bracing pieces until the response becomes distinct.

The bank carries a different rhythm today.

From the far bank the other worker gives a brief answering signal. The signal does not mean agreement. It means that a second observation exists.

Tesh can now compare them.

## 2. At the distance of a hand

The tool does not fit a human palm. It has no straight handle with a cylindrical grip designed for an opposable thumb.

One surface is rough, another gradually curved, and the narrow edge receives pressure from a claw whose keratin tip must not cut through the binding. The piece was shaped with heat, abrasion, controlled drying, and patient selection of grain. It works because a flaw in a larger piece of material has been found and used, not because the worker commands solid wood to change.

Tesh supports the piece against the body and brings one digit across it. Another digit holds the far end. The remaining working point moves minutely, testing whether the material has begun to slide.

A younger learner imitates the action and the piece twists.

The learner makes a sound of irritation and reaches again.

Tesh withdraws, moves a small stone under the work, and repeats the procedure with less hand force. The stone supports the load. The tool stops twisting.

The learner watches a second time and tries again.

The social lesson is not obedience. Tesh has shown a correction that the younger worker can reproduce independently. If the correction fails, the learner will be allowed to say so.

This is one way their technology grows.

## 3. The nursery is not one person's room

The safest path through the settlement runs above the active drainage ground. It was made with little symmetry, because it follows where the bank can carry repeated loads and where the water has been allowed to leave.

At one turn Tesh pauses. A keeper has laid down a boundary cue and changed the air screen beside the nursery. The cue is not a locked door. It is a request for anyone entering to acknowledge the change before crossing the threshold.

An egg-bearing adult approaches by the broader way. Tesh remains aside until the passage is clear.

The bearer is not assigned the only responsibility for the young. Others maintain food, inspect air, prepare the protected surfaces and teach those who will hatch to recognize the same environment. The exact biology of the retained eggs is too intimate and complicated for an observer to reconstruct from a walk through the settlement. The community knows its practice; a distant historian does not.

Tesh checks the air screen, announces the result through a repeated contact pattern, and waits for the keeper's independent confirmation.

There is no ceremony of reverence. Yet the act is repeated whenever an occupied space changes. In another generation, people may tell a story about the first keeper who refused a dangerous passage. They may call it sacred. The practical check must still work.

## 4. At the distance of disagreement

By the time the workers gather near the sounding ground, Tesh has decided that the wet bank should be taken out of service before the next heavy rain.

The surface is not failing now. Most of those present can cross it without noticing anything unusual.

The first opposing observer produces a different interpretation of the stave response. A change in resonance may be caused by an upstream variation in flow, not an imminent collapse. Another worker brings a pattern from a living cultivation patch whose moisture response has changed during the same period.

Tesh offers the original report and a proposed restriction.

A third worker refuses to close the route without examining an alternate crossing. Closing this route would force the nursery workers to carry supplies along a longer path where the ground has never been tested under such frequent loads.

There is no single raised throne, no row of petitioners, no proof that one body type was born to rule.

The disagreement concerns consequences. A decision meant to avoid harm in one place can relocate the harm elsewhere.

They separate the claims: observed change, inferred danger, proposed action, affected people. One observer returns to the river. Another examines the alternate passage. Tesh remains with the learner to teach the test that led to the first warning.

The council delays its decision.

Tesh is unhappy.

The delay may save lives.

## 5. At the distance of an unclosed account

Near evening, one of the people trained in altered-state observation arrives with a report from an occupied Harvest ground.

The witness describes a moment in which the bank seemed both present and absent, the familiar path giving the sensory impression of a place after water had removed it.

Tesh knows the physical warning already. A person trying to flatter Tesh might call the report confirmation.

Tesh does not.

The witness is asked to give the account in the same sequence as it was first remembered, before hearing the river observations. A second custodian keeps that account in a different medium. The two records will be compared later.

The witness is neither mocked nor obeyed.

Tesh has seen too many ordinary signs produce incorrect certainty. The witness has experienced too many unusual impressions to pretend that nothing happened. Both regard the record as valuable.

A young learner asks whether this means that Alchul wants the bank destroyed.

No one answers that question as though they have been present inside Alchul's mind.

A caretaker says that, whatever the origin of the impression, the route can be inspected tomorrow. The living site is still theirs to manage.

Tesh touches the first marker of a failed experiment performed the season before. It has not been erased. The flawed method is kept so the next observer can recognize the same error.

The learner turns from the witness to the old mark and back again.

What the learner is being taught is not an article of faith or a command to doubt every experience. It is a way of remaining responsible while more than one explanation is possible.

The river continues.

The settlement remains inhabited.

The future has not yet been decided by anyone present.

## Four ways to stage this in 3D

**Exterior form:** a full-body biped with a theropod-derived but evolved balance plan; no fixed metric dimensions and no generic human skeleton.

**Working view:** three-digit manipulative contact with irregular tools; show weight borne through stone, bracing and material rather than magical force.

**Social view:** paths, air screens, others' movement constraints, and disagreements grounded in an actual change of the world.

**Contact-testimony view:** a witness and a record—not a visible body or intention belonging to Alchul.

The five scenes are not canon; they are tests of whether a proposed biology can plausibly generate a lived culture.
