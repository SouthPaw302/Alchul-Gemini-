# Canon

## Authority

This file contains locked in-universe facts. Real-world evidence belongs in `RESEARCH/`. Story prose belongs in `WRITING/`. When prose, research notes, and canon conflict, this file controls the fictional universe unless the user explicitly changes canon.

## Founder population

- The founding population is terrestrial Earth life.
- It descends from a Late Cretaceous Appalachian maniraptoran / dromaeosaur-grade theropod population associated with the northern Mississippi Embayment / future New Madrid region.
- The exact founder taxon is not given a modern scientific species name.
- The founders are feathered, predatory, grasping-forelimbed theropods with strong sensory ability, behavioral flexibility, and meaningful juvenile learning.
- Their baseline sociality is facultative rather than wolf-pack-like: solitary behavior, pair/kin tolerance, parental association, temporary aggregation, and opportunistic cooperation.
- They are hunters first. Their later culture never ceases to carry predatory cognition: attention, pursuit, patience, terrain control, ambush logic, memory, and precision.

## The Harvest

- Naturally fermented plant material becomes a recurring ecological resource for a localized population.
- Repeated Harvest gatherings gradually alter social behavior, learning, tolerance, ritual, and selection.
- Fermentation is not itself supernatural.
- Altered states become one of the first reliable conditions under which certain individuals perceive Alchul or effects associated with Alchul.
- Over deep time, the Harvest becomes chemistry, medicine, ritual, experimental method, and eventually part of a larger science.

## Alchul

- Alchul is real within the story.
- Alchul does not exist within the ordinary perceptual bandwidth of surface biological life.
- Alchul's true nature, origin, sex/gender, body, number, and ontology remain unresolved.
- Alchul is not to be reduced to a standard alien, demon, deity, machine, or extra-dimensional species.
- Alchul initially influences through perception, dreams, repeated patterns, intuitions, altered-state contact, and ideas rather than direct technological gifts.
- Alchul does not build the civilization for them. The population develops through experience, selection, experimentation, failure, culture, and long spans of time.
- Some individuals eventually become more naturally sensitive to Alchul's side and function as what a human observer might call shamans, though their own role combines religion, perception, experimentation, medicine, and science.

## Science, religion, and perception

- Their religion and science develop together and are never cleanly separated in the human historical sense.
- Certain individuals can perceive localized displacement of place and time: glimpses of the same location in another moment or condition.
- These experiences can be dreams, impressions, shared states, or more controlled perception.
- Over long periods, repeatable phenomena are studied and systematized.
- Their civilization learns that ordinary biological senses are filters rather than complete representations of reality.
- They eventually learn to travel into or through what they call, in translation, the "other side" or "other sides."
- The transition is gradual: perception first, controlled interaction later, then displacement of matter non-locally.

## Early technical culture

- Their technology evolves from their own body plan and ecology, not from human templates.
- Early capabilities include advanced hunting systems, traps, bait, preservation, fermentation, controlled fire, containers, mineral use, chemistry, smoke control, terrain engineering, and accumulated group knowledge.
- Fire becomes clean, controlled, and technically useful long before the descent.
- Their greatest early geological ability is not telekinesis or brute-force rock movement.
- They develop an increasingly refined capacity to sense where matter is already under stress: fractures, pressure, heat, gas, water movement, unstable seams, and failure points.
- A useful human analogy is an expert miner with an internal canary and an embodied geological sense.
- They exploit weaknesses rather than overpowering stone.
- Tools, heat, cooling, wedges, fluids, pressure, and later more advanced methods do the mechanical work.

## Peak surface civilization

- The final several millennia before sustained migration into the deep represent the peak of their surface civilization. The exact absolute date of this period remains unresolved.
- By this stage they are already a mature localized civilization with cumulative technical knowledge, specialist roles, sophisticated environmental control, and repeatable Alchul-linked practices.
- Their sensory evolution is their own achievement. Long before anomalous perception becomes reliable, natural selection and culture are already favoring extreme attention to weak environmental information, multisensory integration, pattern recognition, memory, and learned prediction.
- Alchul exploits and nudges that existing trajectory. Alchul does not create their intelligence, sensory sophistication, or technical culture from nothing.
- By a human analogy this civilization may sit near a cusp-industrial threshold, but its industry does not reproduce the human path of standardized factories, fossil fuels, mass metallurgy, and electronics.
- The surface civilization never passes through a human-style Iron Age. Material engineering develops around sensing and working existing properties of stone, wood, water, heat, pressure, chemistry, organisms, and environmental systems. This does not settle whether isolated metals were ever used.
- Surface technology increasingly works by cultivating or redirecting natural process: fire, fermentation, water, pressure, heat, gas, mineral chemistry, structural stress, living systems, and ecological feedback.
- Some tools and structures are manufactured objects; others are modified landscapes, cultivated organisms, managed chemical systems, or natural structures made functional through repeated intervention.
- The distinction between natural system and technical system has already begun to blur before the Long Descent.
- This resemblance to nature is primarily functional rather than camouflage.
- Once surface installations are abandoned, many are progressively absorbed into erosion, vegetation, sedimentation, groundwater alteration, mineralization, and other ordinary geological processes.
- The later K–Pg catastrophe and roughly 66 million years of subsequent geology further destroy, bury, alter, or decontextualize the remaining surface record.
- A surviving trace of this civilization need not look like a machine to a modern investigator. It may first present as geology, ecology, combustion residue, an anomalous mineral association, an altered water system, or an apparently natural structure.
- By the peak surface period, egg-bearing descendants retain developing eggs internally for approximately three quarters of embryonic development before laying them for the remaining external incubation. The exact anatomy, oxygen-transfer mechanism, shell chemistry, clutch size, and timing remain subjects for biological reconstruction.
- Exact population scale, city form, extent of non-ferrous or incidental metal use, durable record-keeping media, and the degree of centralized organization remain open until separately reconstructed.

## The coming catastrophe

- By repeated localized perception across time and place, some of them become aware that a planetary catastrophe is coming.
- The knowledge is incomplete, debated, culturally interpreted, and accumulated over many generations.
- They do not immediately understand "asteroid impact" in modern human terms.
- Visions include fire, abnormal sky brightness, darkness, cold, ecological collapse, and the disappearance of surface life.
- Their response is active study, debate, preparation, and eventually relocation.

## The Long Descent

- The descent begins only after the civilization has already developed substantial technical, scientific, religious, social, and Alchul-linked capabilities.
- They do not become civilized because they enter caves.
- They enter the deep deliberately because they are already capable of surveying, sensing, modifying, and surviving increasingly difficult environments.
- Migration underground happens slowly, in many waves, across immense spans of time.
- Surface and subsurface populations coexist for long periods.
- Some groups never make it to the deepest regions.
- Some groups are lost or isolated.
- Some individuals are born below and never personally know the open sky.
- The final abandonment of the surface is a civilizational relocation, not a single exodus event.

## The deep world

- The first underground environments are natural, discontinuous, wet, fractured, chemically rich, dangerous, and only locally habitable.
- The inhabitants deliberately transform the places they enter.
- They alter light, airflow, water, heat, chemistry, microbial/fungal ecology, gas use, surfaces, and living systems.
- The luminous deep world is therefore not simply found ready-made. It becomes increasingly luminous and habitable because natural bioluminescent/geochemical phenomena are cultivated, amplified, bred, engineered, and integrated into infrastructure over immense spans of time.
- Their settlements are partly carved, partly grown, partly cultivated, partly ecological, and partly geological.
- Natural gas, geothermal energy, groundwater, minerals, chemical gradients, and later more advanced energy systems become resources.
- The deep world contains natural biospheres and lineages that developed separately from the surface in relative isolation. The degree of independent origin versus deep common ancestry remains open inside the story unless later canon resolves it.

## Civilization after K–Pg

- The civilization survives the K–Pg surface catastrophe.
- The end of the surface world is not the end of their civilization.
- Over the following ~66 million years, descendants diverge biologically, culturally, cognitively, and technologically.
- Natural evolution continues, but advanced civilization eventually gains deliberate control over heredity and form.
- The ancestral theropod body plan becomes a deep biological inheritance rather than a frozen visual template.
- Multiple lineages and morphologies emerge.
- Some present-day forms may partially converge on efficient upright, manipulative body plans also seen in humans, but they are not humans, hybrids, or imitations.
- Long observation of mammals and later humans can influence deliberate design choices, but their biology remains terrestrial theropod-derived.

## Communication and cognition

- Their communication is multimodal and nonhuman.
- It can include vocal sound, posture, feather/filament display, chemical signaling, touch, vibration, spatial arrangement, ritual states, and eventually direct or group-linked cognition.
- Writing is not required for civilization if information can be preserved through chemical, biological, mnemonic, environmental, collective, or other media.
- Knowledge systems can be living rather than textual.
- A settlement, cultivated ecology, mineral structure, fungal culture, or synchronized group may function as archive, computation, communication, or machine.

## Technology

- Their mature technology is not a more advanced copy of human industrial technology.
- Their machines can be ecosystems.
- Their infrastructure can be rock, fluid, gas, mineral, living tissue, fungi, heat, pressure, resonance, chemistry, cognition, and field effects arranged into functional systems.
- Their technological lineage proceeds from perception of local displacement to interaction across boundaries, then small matter displacement, living matter displacement, and eventually non-local displacement of large systems.
- Their mature transport does not necessarily cross every intervening kilometer conventionally.
- A vessel is better understood as a portable engineered environment capable of establishing the conditions for displacement.
- Their spacefaring vessels can resemble natural rocks, asteroids, carbonaceous bodies, or mineral masses because rough stone is functional structure, shielding, habitat, and machinery—not merely camouflage.
- They colonize interiors, fractures, moons, asteroids, and subsurface environments rather than defaulting to surface terraforming.
- Their present civilization is approximately Type 1.7-equivalent only as a rough human energy comparison. Kardashev classification does not describe their actual social or technological structure.

## Present day

- The New Madrid population remains alive.
- It is part of a much wider civilization that has spread through Earth and beyond.
- Modern New Madrid seismic activity is connected in-universe to their deep civilization and infrastructure.
- Surface instruments generally interpret effects through ordinary geology because the underlying infrastructure is integrated with geology.
- Human civilization is a very late surface development from their perspective.
- They have observed mammals, primates, and humans over long spans of time.
- Some influence on human culture may have occurred, but the nature and extent remain unresolved.
- Human serpent/reptilian/underworld traditions may preserve distorted echoes of encounters, but no specific myth is automatically literalized.

## Visual canon

- Founder forms must remain strongly theropod and feathered.
- Modern descendants must not be depicted as generic reptilian humanoids, raptors in robes, or humans with scales.
- Present forms should reflect ~66 million years of evolution, divergence, civilization, and directed biological change.
- Their ancestry should survive in deep anatomical logic, not necessarily in obvious superficial resemblance.
